> How the process ran from 2026-11-20 to 2026-11-22, from the numbers `/sdlc:close` recorded on each
> `Close` commit, and the host's own figures from its `retro.indicators`. It opens with the
> scorecard: the Value band, each pillar's headline and guardrail against the last retrospective,
> and the Trust band. Then the insights: each synthesis question answered from its pillar's
> figures, as approved at the retrospective's question. Then the experience: how working with the
> process felt, the person's rating from 1 to 4 and their line, given at that question. Each
> pillar's figures follow in a section of its own, then the hosts: the counts from other repos
> using this process, read only where the process itself is built. `sdlc.py retro-commit` writes
> it and commits it with the retrospective; `sdlc.py retro` gives the same figures, and the next
> retrospective reads back the scorecard's table, the experience's and the indicators'.

### Cost: Little of the spend is wasted (7% of dollars, 13% of minutes). Most of the waste is rework, and the clearest saving that costs no time is the security reviewer, the only reviewer still on Opus, which costs about three times as much per run as the reviewers on Sonnet.

*Where is spend wasted, and what would cost less without slowing anything?*

- Evidence: Two things changed underneath before any trend can be read: plugin 1.10.0 to 1.11.0 (26 of 34 changes ran on 1.11.0) and Claude Code 2.1.323 (2 of 34); the models did not change. So the rises in the medians, tier 1 $2.36 against $2.03 last window (n 17) and tier 2 $7.38 against $7.30 (n 17), should not be blamed on the process. Total spend is $190.19 (n 34, all measured, not greyed). Opus accounts for $158.99 of it over 104 runs, Sonnet for $31.20 over 227 runs. The builder alone costs $122.01 (54 runs across 34 changes) and the spec-writer $24.07 (18 runs). Waste is $12.60 of $190.19 (7%) and 140.8 of 1,088.5 minutes (13%), n 34. Within it, rework is $8.51 and 83.7 minutes (23 runs), mend is $1.46 but 52.2 minutes (6 runs), ejected is $2.45 (10 runs) and gate-fix $0.18 (1 run). The security reviewer on Opus costs $10.92 over 26 runs across 16 changes, about $0.42 a run. The Sonnet reviewers cost about $0.15 a run for code ($7.68, 51 runs), $0.13 for behaviour ($9.64, 77 runs) and $0.16 for test ($5.73, 36 runs). The approver already moved mostly to Sonnet (28 changes, 33 runs) from Opus (6 changes, 6 runs). The window's findings counts are all 0, so these figures cannot show whether a cheaper reviewer would catch less.
- Recommendation: Trial the security reviewer on Sonnet for the next window, as was done for the approver, and compare its cost per run and its findings with the Opus runs before keeping the switch. At this window's rates that saves about $7 (around 4% of spend) without adding a step. Rework is the larger waste, but it comes down by raising first-time build rates (tier 2 0.5, tier 1 0.65), which is a Quality question, not a Cost one.
- Disposition: seed 2026-11-22-47
